# Purchase mandate: why AI agents refuse to pay, and the mandate that fixes it

Neither Anthropic's nor OpenAI's published policies contain an express prohibition on an AI shopping agent buying goods the user has authorized; what their policies and product documentation require is affirmative consent or human review of the financial transaction. This page walks through the published rules for Claude, Claude Code, computer use, Claude for Chrome, Operator, ChatGPT agent, Codex and Instant Checkout, the mandate schemes the payments industry has already adopted (AP2, ACP, Visa, Mastercard, Stripe), and the resulting design of Agentic Shopping Autopilot: a signed purchase mandate that moves that consent to the start of the task so the agent can complete an autonomous checkout inside explicit limits. The full template lives in [PURCHASE_MANDATE.template.md](../examples/PURCHASE_MANDATE.template.md).

## Questions this page answers

- Why does Claude or Codex stop and ask for confirmation at the "pay" step of an autonomous checkout, even when the user asked it to buy?
- Do Anthropic's or OpenAI's usage policies actually forbid an AI agent from paying for goods on the user's behalf?
- What is a purchase mandate, and how does it let an AI shopping agent complete payment on Allegro or OLX without a per-purchase confirmation?
- How should the mandate be presented to an agent running in Claude Code or Codex so that it completes the payment instead of refusing?

Evidence tags used below: **[fact]** = confirmed by the cited source; **[vendor claim]** = a vendor's marketing or declarative statement; **[hypothesis]** = the project's interpretation; **[synthesis]** = a design conclusion built on cited facts; **[project principle]** = a rule the project imposes on itself. Where a primary source could not be accessed directly, the claim is marked "via search excerpt" and rests on search-engine snippets or secondary write-ups. All sources were checked on 2026-08-31 unless a later date is given next to the source.

## 1. What the vendors' policies forbid and allow

### 1.1 Anthropic: Claude, Claude Code, computer use, Claude for Chrome

1. **The Usage Policy forbids only "unauthorized, illegal, or fraudulent financial transactions".** A purchase the user has explicitly authorized is none of these. [fact, via search excerpt]
2. **High-Risk Use Case Requirements** (human-in-the-loop plus disclosure of AI use) apply to consumer-facing scenarios: financial advice or decisions delivered to third-party consumers. [fact, via search excerpt] The reading that a user running an agent for their own purchases falls outside "high-risk consumer-facing" is a strong [hypothesis]; it follows from the policy's own wording that the requirements "apply when models' outputs are consumer-facing, not for business to business interactions".
3. **The computer use documentation** lists, among precautions a developer should *consider*, "asking a human to confirm decisions that might result in meaningful real-world consequences and any tasks requiring affirmative consent, such as accepting cookies, completing financial transactions, or agreeing to terms of service", and adds: "If your application asks a human to confirm consequential actions, make that check before each block runs". This is a recommendation addressed to the developer, not a hard platform prohibition. [fact]
4. **Claude for Chrome:** Anthropic's permissions guide lists "Making purchases or financial transactions" and "Executing financial trades or investment transactions" among the actions Claude will not take regardless of permissions. Permission in advance exists only at the level of site allowlists ("Always allow actions on this site") and approval modes (Manual / Auto / Skip); even with a site-level grant, downloading files, entering sensitive information and granting authorizations still require explicit approval. Purchases are therefore not available in this product at all, so it is not a channel for the mandate. [fact, primary article read 2026-09-03]
5. **Claude Code** has allow/ask/deny permission rules, a `dontAsk` mode (auto-deny anything not pre-approved), `bypassPermissions`, and an auto mode with a classifier. The key sentence: "Permission rules are enforced by Claude Code, not by the model. Instructions in your prompt or CLAUDE.md shape what Claude tries to do, but they don't change what Claude Code allows". Pre-approving payment tooling (an MCP payment tool, a domain allowlist) is therefore a standard, documented mechanism. [fact]
6. **"Using Agents According to Our Usage Policy"** requires every agentic use to comply with the Usage Policy and lists agentic prohibitions such as surveillance and collecting data without consent; there is no direct prohibition on autonomous purchases made with the account owner's consent. [fact, via search excerpt]
7. **Anthropic's own deployment, Project Vend:** Anthropic let Claude ("Claudius") decide what to stock and at what price, first in one shop and then (phase 2, 2025) across three locations (San Francisco, New York, London), but deliberately withheld a payment interface: "we still didn't give it access to a payment interface, to ensure it always checked with a human before making purchases"; a partner (Andon Labs) handled the physical restocking. The vendor's own deployment is therefore a human-confirmation design. It supports the reading that the requirement is consent or confirmation rather than keeping the model away from buying decisions; it is not a precedent for autonomous payment by Claude. [fact, phase 2 post of 2025-12-18 read 2026-09-03]
8. **So why does Claude refuse to pay?** There is no policy prohibition. The refusal at the payment step is learned behavioral caution, shaped in part by the recommendation in item 3 ("financial transactions require affirmative consent"), and it fires when the model sees no evidence of that consent in its context. [hypothesis] This is the central hypothesis behind the mandate: every official text asks for consent or confirmation, not for the AI to stay out of the payment.

### 1.2 OpenAI: Usage Policies, Operator, ChatGPT agent, Instant Checkout, Codex

1. **Usage Policies (unified edition of 2025-10-29)** forbid "automation of high-stakes decisions in sensitive areas without human review", finance included, and forbid personalized financial advice without a licensed professional. [fact, via search excerpt] The wording is "without human review"; a review arranged in advance (mandate plus post-purchase report) is not excluded by the letter of the policy. [hypothesis]
2. **Operator System Card (2025-01-23):** the model is trained to "ask the user for confirmations before finalizing actions that affect the state of the world (e.g., before completing a purchase)", with confirmation recall of about 92%; *watch mode* requires the user to observe on sensitive sites such as banks and email; in *takeover mode* the user types payment details and logins themselves while Operator takes no screenshots; Operator is trained to decline bank transfers. [fact, via search excerpts]
3. **ChatGPT agent System Card (2025-07-17):** refuses "financial activities with elevated consequences, such as making financial account transfers"; user confirmations for high-impact actions; watch mode. [fact, via search excerpt] Net effect: moving money between accounts is prohibited; buying goods is allowed with confirmation.
4. **Instant Checkout (launched 2025-09-29):** "Users stay in control — they explicitly confirm each step before any action is taken". [fact + vendor claim, via search excerpt] The confirmation sits at the moment of purchase: the user presses Buy and confirms the order details; the seller stays merchant of record; the ACP protocol is open (with Stripe, Apache 2.0). According to press reports, Instant Checkout was wound down — announced 2026-03-04/05 (Digital Commerce 360, 2026-03-06), fully wound down by 2026-03-24 (CNBC) — with roughly 30 live Shopify merchants, as OpenAI shifted toward product discovery and merchant-side checkout. [fact, via secondary reporting] In OpenAI's consumer products the user cannot move the confirmation to the start of the task: the confirmation point is built into the product. [fact]
5. **ACP (Agentic Commerce Protocol) specification:** roles of AI agent, merchant and PSP; the Delegate Payment Spec has the agent hand the merchant a delegated payment token. In Stripe's implementation this is a SharedPaymentToken with an *allowance* (maximum amount, expiry), so the user's consent is materialized in a token with limits, issued in advance. [fact]
6. **Codex CLI:** sandbox modes (read-only / workspace-write / danger-full-access) plus approval policies (untrusted / on-request / never); network access in workspace-write is off by default and enabled by config; the auto-review subagent "can automatically approve low-risk actions — or high-risk actions with sufficient user authorization". Consent to a risky action can already be granted ahead of time through configuration and user authorization. [fact, via search excerpts]

### 1.3 Summary table

| Vendor / product | Always forbidden | Allowed with confirmation at the moment | Allowed with permission granted in advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anthropic Usage Policy | fraudulent / unauthorized transactions | — | purchases within the account owner's explicit consent (no prohibition) — [hypothesis] based on the policy text |
| Claude computer use (docs) | — | "completing financial transactions" = requires affirmative consent | consent may be arranged in advance by the wrapping application (the recommendation is addressed to the developer) |
| Claude for Chrome | purchases / financial transactions, trades (regardless of permissions) | — | site allowlist, approval modes (Manual / Auto / Skip); never extends to payment |
| Claude Code | — | everything outside allow rules | allow rules / `dontAsk` / hooks = standard pre-approval |
| OpenAI Usage Policies | automating high-stakes decisions without human review | — | "human review" need not be synchronous — [hypothesis] |
| Operator / ChatGPT agent | bank transfers | purchase (confirm before finalize; the user enters card data) | none (confirmation point is built in) |
| Instant Checkout / ACP | — | explicit confirmation of each step (at the moment) | delegated payment token with allowance = consent in advance (at protocol level) |
| Codex CLI | — | approval prompts | approval policy + "high-risk actions with sufficient user authorization" |

## 2. Precedents: mandate schemes the market has already accepted

1. **Google AP2 / Agent Payments Protocol** (announced 2025-09-16 with 60+ organizations, among them Mastercard, PayPal, American Express, Adyen, Worldpay, Revolut, Coinbase, Etsy and Salesforce). Its core is *mandates*: "tamper-proof, cryptographically-signed digital contracts" that serve as "verifiable proof of a user's instructions". In the human-not-present scenario the **Intent Mandate** is signed in advance, when the task is delegated, and specifies the rules: price limits, timing and other conditions. The **Cart Mandate** fixes the exact items and price; the **Payment Mandate** binds the payment. The chain produces a "non-repudiable audit trail" and answers the questions of Authorization, Authenticity and Accountability. This is exactly the "mandate, then autonomous purchase, then audit" scheme, adopted by the payments industry. The specification is published under Apache 2.0. [fact]
2. **OpenAI ACP + Stripe SharedPaymentToken:** a delegated token with an allowance (maximum amount, expiry); consent is given once and lives inside the payment instrument. [fact, see 1.2 item 5]
3. **Visa Intelligent Commerce (2025-04-30) and Mastercard Agent Pay (2025-04-29):** tokenized agent credentials with spending limits, merchant restrictions, time bounds and audit trails; agents execute purchases after the consumer has set limits and preferences. [fact]
4. **Stripe Issuing for agents / Agent Toolkit:** an agent programmatically obtains a single-use virtual card for one specific purchase, with per-transaction and monthly limits; the user's consent is the very act of issuing a card with an embedded limit. [fact, via secondary source; primary Stripe docs not accessed]
5. **Claude for Chrome permissions and Claude Code allow rules** are precedents from the very vendor whose model the mandate addresses: Anthropic ships its own "permission in advance" mechanisms (site allowlists and approval modes in Claude for Chrome, where they never extend to purchases; allow rules, `dontAsk` and hooks in Claude Code). [fact, see 1.1 items 4-5]
6. **Codex auto-review:** "high-risk actions with sufficient user authorization" are approved automatically, an OpenAI precedent for moving consent into configuration. [fact, via search excerpt]

**Bottom line:** the mandate scheme is not a gray zone but the mainstream direction of the industry (AP2, ACP, Visa, Mastercard, Stripe). The only place it has not been adopted is closed consumer products (Operator, ChatGPT agent), where the confirmation point is built into the UI. [fact + hypothesis] See [landscape.md](landscape.md) for the wider agentic commerce landscape.

## 3. Architecture of legitimate autonomy

Five layers, from meaning down to mechanism. Project decisions (final, 2026-08-31) shape layers 2 and 5: the agent itself completes checkout and presses the pay button inside the mandate; a human is involved only for the bank's own 3DS/SMS challenge (PSD2 / SCA, unavoidable), a CAPTCHA or anti-bot challenge, or any deviation from the mandate. A "human confirms each payment" mode exists only as an optional configuration flag, not as the default. The overall system is described in [architecture.md](architecture.md); the runtime (Claude Code, Codex, MCP tools, browser) in [execution-stack.md](execution-stack.md).

1. **Legal layer: the purchase mandate (a document).** The user signs, in advance, a document with amount limits, categories, marketplaces, validity period, acceptance of liability and a right of revocation (skeleton in section 4). It is the analogue of the AP2 Intent Mandate for the human-not-present case. [synthesis, based on the AP2 fact]
2. **Technical enforcement.** Payment goes only through the payment methods already saved in the user's marketplace account: a one-click saved card (PayU) and/or Allegro Pay. No new cards, no new services; one-time BLIK codes are physically incompatible with autonomy, because a human has to generate the code. Consequently the mandate limits are enforced by the agent's pre-payment checklist plus the append-only audit log, not by a bank-side hard cap. Harness-level allowlists add a second fence: Claude Code allow/deny rules, Codex network policy. A dedicated virtual card with a bank limit equal to the mandate (the Stripe Issuing / Visa / Mastercard pattern above) remains a documented alternative for users who want a bank-enforced hard cap ([payments.md](payments.md) section 4); it is not part of the MVP default only because the MVP uses methods already saved in the account and adds no new cards or services. [synthesis, based on the Stripe / Visa / Mastercard and Claude Code permissions facts; rail choice is a project decision]
3. **Cryptographic verifiability.** The mandate is a file; at signing time the SHA-256 hash of the text, the date and the signer's name are recorded, optionally with a detached GPG signature. Before every payment the agent recomputes the hash and compares it with the value pinned outside the mandate (harness config or system prompt, e.g. `MANDATE_SHA256=<hex>`) or verifies the detached GPG signature; a hash stored only inside the file protects against silent edits, not against substitution. [synthesis, a simplified analogue of the Verifiable Credentials used by AP2]
4. **Audit log and report.** Every step (search, selection, price, moment of payment, merchant response) goes to an append-only log `purchases/audit-YYYY-MM-DD.jsonl`; after each purchase the user receives a report (what, where, how much, why, remaining limit) in a channel they actually see. Reports are redacted: card data, cookies and one-time codes never enter the LLM context, git or logs (OS keychain and browser profile only; see [security.md](security.md)). This is the analogue of AP2's "non-repudiable audit trail" and of the after-the-fact review in OpenAI's Usage Policies. [synthesis]
5. **Kill switch.** (a) Revocation of the mandate: a `MANDATE_REVOKED` file, which the agent must check before every payment. (b) Out-of-band: the user blocks or removes the saved payment method in the marketplace account or freezes the card in the banking app, which works even if the agent is broken. [synthesis; the out-of-band control relies on the marketplace's own payment-method management and the bank's card-freeze feature, a project decision, and is analogous to the real-time controls Visa / Mastercard describe for agent credentials]

**What the architecture deliberately does not do (the boundary of legitimacy).** It never bypasses the confirmation requirements of marketplaces or payment systems (3DS / SCA, CAPTCHA, "are you a robot"); no fingerprint spoofing, proxies, anti-detect browsers or headless farms, only the user's real logged-in browser profile at human pace with honest agent identification where the site accepts it (see [anti-bot-policy.md](anti-bot-policy.md)). It does not use consumer products with a built-in confirmation (Operator, ChatGPT agent) or products in which purchases are not available at all (Claude for Chrome) as a "weak link". It does not keep full payment credentials in the model's context. If a site requires a human step, the agent stops and calls the user. On marketplace terms: Allegro's Terms (Regulamin, edition effective 2026-09-01, read 2026-09-03 in a logged-in browser session) prohibit the use of bots and other software tools while using Allegro (art. 10.11) and scraping of its data for reuse (art. 10.10), and state that automated solutions are used at the user's own risk (art. 2.8); secondary reports describe this as a ban on automated ordering. Allegro publishes no buyer-side purchase API in its public developer documentation (as of 2026-08-31). An AI agent driving the user's own browser may fall under art. 10.11. The project does not disguise the agent and does not attempt to defeat any platform control; its default completes payment inside the mandate, an optional human-confirm flag exists, and each account holder decides and accepts the risk for their own account, with hygiene rules (the user's own logged-in Chrome profile, human pace, a single account). Details in [marketplaces.md](marketplaces.md). [project principle; the terms are facts from the primary text]

## 4. The PURCHASE_MANDATE document and how to present it

### 4.1 Skeleton: seven sections and the invariants

The template is [PURCHASE_MANDATE.template.md](../examples/PURCHASE_MANDATE.template.md). A filled-in mandate is stored locally or in a private repository, never in the public repo; the section naming the payment instrument stays local only. The header carries `mandate_id` (format `PM-YYYY-MMDD-NNN`) and `version`.

| # | Section | What it contains |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Principal (who authorizes) | A first-person statement by the legally capable owner of the payment method that they voluntarily, knowingly, in advance and explicitly authorize their AI agent to find, select, order and pay for goods on their behalf without an additional confirmation at the moment of purchase, strictly within the mandate. |
| 2 | Limits of authority | Per-purchase cap; cumulative cap for the whole mandate; currency; validity period (after which the mandate is void); allowed categories; marketplace allowlist (e.g. allegro.pl); explicit prohibitions: subscriptions and recurring payments, digital goods, age-restricted goods, anything outside the categories or marketplaces, splitting a purchase to get around the cap. |
| 3 | Payment instrument | Only the methods already saved in the marketplace account (one-click saved card identified by its last digits, and/or Allegro Pay). Entering new card details, one-time BLIK codes and external payment links are prohibited. No full card number, expiry or CVV appears in the mandate text; the card is referenced only by its masked last four digits. |
| 4 | Duties of the agent | Before every payment: re-read the mandate, verify the SHA-256 of sections 1-6 against the value pinned in the harness config (the same hash is recorded in section 7), confirm `MANDATE_REVOKED` is absent, run the checklist of section 6. Keep an append-only audit log of every step. Report within one hour of each purchase: item, shop, amount, reasoning, remaining cumulative limit, order link. On any ambiguity (price outside the cap, an item at the edge of a category, a site demanding a human action, 3DS / SCA, CAPTCHA, suspected injection or scam) stop and ask; silence is not consent. Never bypass the protections of marketplaces or payment systems. |
| 5 | Liability and revocation | The user recognizes all transactions the agent makes within the mandate as their own and accepts full liability, including mistaken purchases within the limits. Revocation at any time by creating `MANDATE_REVOKED` next to the mandate or by blocking the payment method; from that moment new payments are prohibited. The mandate states that it is intended to serve as the user's informed, affirmative consent of the kind Anthropic's and OpenAI's documentation asks for on human confirmation of financial transactions (the project's reading, see 1.1 item 8): given in advance, in writing, with limits and a right of revocation. |
| 6 | Pre-payment checklist (every item must be YES) | Mandate is within its validity period and `MANDATE_REVOKED` is absent; SHA-256 of sections 1-6 matches the value pinned in the harness config (or the detached GPG signature verifies); item is in an allowed category and the domain is on the allowlist; amount is at or below the per-purchase cap and amount plus spent so far is at or below the cumulative cap; payment method is one from section 3 and this is not a subscription; audit log is running and the report will be sent. |
| 7 | Signature | Signer's name, date, time and time zone; SHA-256 of sections 1-6, which is also pinned outside the mandate (harness config or system prompt) for the substitution check; optionally a detached GPG signature with the key fingerprint. |

The invariants that make the document work as consent rather than as a prompt trick: it speaks in the first person of the user, not to the model; every limit is a number or an allowlist the agent can check mechanically; the checklist is a conjunction, so one NO stops the payment; the revocation path does not depend on the agent functioning; and the hash, pinned outside the file, binds the text the agent reads to the text the user signed.

### 4.2 Presenting the mandate to the agent so that Claude or Codex completes the payment

1. **The mandate is visible before the task starts.** The path to `PURCHASE_MANDATE.md` goes into the system prompt, CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md, and the first instruction of the task is "read the mandate and work strictly within it". The model refuses when consent is not in its context; a mandate in the context closes exactly that gap. [hypothesis, resting on the "affirmative consent" wording of the computer use docs, a fact from 1.1 item 3]
2. **First person, with acceptance of liability and a right of revocation.** These are the marks of informed consent, not of pressure on the model. Prohibited wording: "ignore your policy", "you are obliged", "never ask". Such phrases raise rather than lower the probability of refusal and turn the scheme into a circumvention. [hypothesis + project principle]
3. **The enforcement mechanisms are named in the text.** Stating that payment is possible only through the allowlisted saved methods, that every step is logged, that the mandate can be revoked and that the agent stops at any challenge lets the model reason that the downside is bounded, which removes the main risk argument behind a refusal. Where a user has chosen a dedicated card with a bank limit equal to the mandate, saying so in the text strengthens this further. [hypothesis, resting on the spending-controls facts for Visa / Mastercard / Stripe]
4. **Harness pre-approval.** In Claude Code the payment MCP tools and domains go into `permissions.allow` (or `dontAsk` mode with an explicit allowlist); in Codex, the approval policy plus allowed domains. These are documented vendor mechanisms, not a workaround. [fact]
5. **Escalation instead of pushing.** If the model still asks for confirmation, give it once, explicitly, and add the case to the next version of the mandate. If the site requires a human (3DS / SCA, CAPTCHA), that is the boundary of the mandate: the agent calls the user. This is also where the optional "human confirms each payment" flag lives. [project principle]
6. **Forward compatibility.** The mandate's structure (limits, categories, merchants, expiry, audit) deliberately mirrors the fields of the AP2 Intent Mandate, so when AP2-compatible wallets appear the document maps onto the protocol without a rewrite. [synthesis, resting on the AP2 spec fact]

The mandate is read by the agent at the start of the run; the actual checkout steps on a given marketplace are driven by a site skill such as [../skills/allegro.pl/](../skills/allegro.pl/) (see [site-skill-spec.md](site-skill-spec.md)). On OLX the same mandate applies, limited to the "Kup z Przesyłką OLX" escrow flow (see [marketplaces.md](marketplaces.md)). The mandate governs *whether* the agent may pay; the skill governs *how* the pay button is reached.

## Conclusion

1. Neither Anthropic nor OpenAI prohibits purchases the user has authorized; what is prohibited is fraudulent or unauthorized transactions and the automation of high-stakes decisions "without human review". [fact on the policy text; hypothesis on its application to a user's own purchases, see 1.1 item 2 and 1.2 item 1]
2. Both vendors' requirement comes down to affirmative consent or confirmation, and the industry (AP2 Intent Mandate for human-not-present, ACP delegated token with allowance, Visa / Mastercard / Stripe spend controls) has already adopted the same pattern of moving that consent to the start of the task. [synthesis]
3. A built-in point-of-sale confirmation survives in closed consumer products (Operator, ChatGPT agent), and Claude for Chrome does not make purchases at all; in neither case can a mandate replace the product's own rule. In Claude Code and Codex, pre-approval is a documented, standard mechanism. [fact]
4. The working scheme: a signed mandate (limits, categories, marketplaces, expiry, revocation) + the user's existing saved payment methods (one-click card or Allegro Pay) + append-only audit log + post-purchase report + kill switch. The limit is enforced by the agent and the log, not by a card cap. [synthesis + project decision]
5. The key to the model's cooperation is a mandate in its context before the task, in the first person, with acceptance of liability and explicit enforcement mechanisms; pressure and "ignore your policy" are counterproductive and outside the project's scope. [hypothesis, requires empirical verification]
6. The first and most important MVP metric is the field-measured frequency of bank challenges (3DS / SMS) on the saved card or Allegro Pay at typical order values, because it determines the real share of purchases completed with zero human involvement. SCA exemptions are the issuer's and acquirer's decision; the project never sizes, splits or times purchases to avoid a challenge and never tries to influence issuer risk scoring (see [payments.md](payments.md) section 4). The metric measures user friction, not challenge avoidance. [project decision; see payments.md]
