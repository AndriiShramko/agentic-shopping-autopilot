# User guide: letting your AI agent buy for you on Allegro (and later OLX)

This page is the practical companion to the specifications in this folder. It walks a technical user of Claude Code or Codex through installing the Allegro.pl site skill and the `asa` runtime, signing a purchase mandate, and running the first purchases in your own browser with a payment method you already have. It also describes how the agent consults your Markdown knowledge base (an Obsidian vault or any folder of notes) before every search, so that it already knows what to buy and in which size, colour and quantity, and how it uses Allegro Smart! to reach free delivery — and it explains where the agent stops for you (the bank's 3DS challenge, a CAPTCHA, a deviation from the mandate) and what the honest limits of the whole approach are.

**Status of what this page describes (2026-09-04).** Implemented in [`../runtime/`](../runtime/README.md) and covered by offline tests (`npm test`, no marketplace traffic): the skill, the runtime CLI, the mandate checker with per-item / per-order / aggregate limits, the quick limit change (`asa mandate:amend` → `asa mandate:sign`), the one-time over-limit approval (`asa override`), the domain allowlist, the CDP browser channel, the ten-step checkout with the mandate gate, the recipient check, the audit log and the report. Implemented later the same day and covered by offline tests: the Smart! basket planner (`asa basket:plan` / `asa basket:approve`, `search --need … --append`), the shopping-profile reader (`shopping-profile/*.jsonl`, `asa profile:check`) and the `SMART_*` / `MAX_COMPLEMENTS` / `REORDER_COOLDOWN_DAYS` settings. **Implemented 2026-09-05 and covered by offline tests: context-first** — the runtime reads your knowledge stores itself (`CONTEXT_STORES`), `asa context:brief` / `asa context:note`, the hard gate that stops `asa search` and `asa basket:plan` without a fresh brief, and the purchase history appended by the runtime after every confirmed order (section 4). **Still planned, not in the runtime:** multi-line checkout (one basket line is bought per run today), line-by-line cart verification, delivery-cost reading at checkout, the `smart_threshold_observed` event, the seller-store search for complements and `offer:inspect`. Sections 4 and 7 mark those items *(planned)*. The authoritative list of commands is the usage line printed by `asa` with no arguments. Nothing on this page is a promise about Allegro's behaviour: field observations are dated, and the first MVP metric (how often the bank asks for 3DS on small saved-card payments) is still being measured.

## 1. What you get

**Today.** An AI agent that runs inside the tool you already use (Claude Code or Codex CLI), reads a spending mandate you signed once, and buys one offer per run on Allegro.pl for you: it searches, filters mechanically against the mandate, opens the offer your session selected, completes the checkout in your own logged-in Chrome and presses "Kupuję i płacę" itself with the card or Allegro Pay already saved in your Allegro account. You are involved only to answer a one-time over-limit question (`ok <amount>`), to complete your bank's 3DS/SMS challenge when the bank asks for it, and when the agent hits a CAPTCHA, a page it does not recognise, or a deviation from the mandate. Every step lands in an append-only, redacted audit log and ends with a report: what was bought, for how much, why, how much of the mandate is left, and the order link.

**Context-first.** Before it searches or plans, the agent must consult your own knowledge stores — an Obsidian vault, the shopping-profile files, any folder of notes — for the need at hand: the runtime retrieves the relevant lines itself (`asa context:brief`), the session records the facts it derived and the assumptions it made (`asa context:note`), and `asa search` / `asa basket:plan` refuse to run without that brief. So the agent knows your bed size, your usual colour and what you bought last month without asking; a gap becomes a flagged assumption or an open question in the proposal, never a question to you. It groups items at one seller so that Allegro Smart! makes delivery free and, when it adds a complementary item to reach the threshold, sends you one proposal with the exact totals and waits for your one-word approval. That approval is a cart-mandate step before checkout (AP2 Intent → Cart), not a payment confirmation, and it is required only when a complement is added or an amount exceeds a limit — a single wishlist line within the limits runs with a human involved only on the bank's challenge, an anti-bot challenge or a deviation ([security.md](security.md), scope).

**Questions this page answers**

- How do I install an Allegro shopping skill into Claude Code or Codex and let the agent complete the payment by itself?
- How do I authorize an AI agent to spend my money with limits per item, per order and in total, and how do I revoke that authorization instantly?
- How can an AI shopping agent know my sizes, brands, quantities and past purchases without asking me clarifying questions every time? *(implemented: section 4, context-first)*
- Which browser and which payment method does the agent use on Allegro, and do I need a new card or a new account for it?
- How will the agent reach the Allegro Smart! free-delivery threshold, and when exactly does it stop and hand a purchase back to me?

## 2. What a site skill is and how to install it

A **site skill** is a folder in the open Agent Skills (`SKILL.md`) format that packages everything the agent needs to know about one marketplace: when to trigger, how to search, how to read a product page, how to fill the cart, how to run checkout and pay, how to track the order, and the hard rules (allowed domains, allowed payment methods, what counts as a stop). The format is specified in [site-skill-spec.md](site-skill-spec.md); the reference skill is [`../skills/allegro.pl/`](../skills/allegro.pl/) (`SKILL.md`, `selectors.yaml`, `flows/`). Because Claude Code and Codex both read the same standard, one folder serves both agents. A skill never contains or sees payment data: the runtime triggers the payment method saved in your marketplace account, and the skill only describes the flow.

**Install the skill (today, by copying):**

```bash
git clone https://github.com/AndriiShramko/agentic-shopping-autopilot
# Claude Code
cp -r agentic-shopping-autopilot/skills/allegro.pl ~/.claude/skills/allegro.pl
# Codex CLI (also reads .agents/skills inside a repository)
cp -r agentic-shopping-autopilot/skills/allegro.pl ~/.agents/skills/allegro.pl
```

A one-command installer (`npx skills add …` compatibility and the project's own `skill add <site>`) is a v1 item, see [registry.md](registry.md).

**Install the runtime** (Node 20 + TypeScript + Playwright; full details in [`../runtime/README.md`](../runtime/README.md)):

```bash
cd agentic-shopping-autopilot/runtime
npm install
npx playwright install chromium   # only for the offline fixture tests; the real runs use YOUR Chrome
npm test                          # offline tests, no marketplace traffic
npm run build                     # then: node dist/cli.js <command>, or npm run cli -- <command>
```

The runtime keeps everything private outside the public repository, in a **private folder** (`ASA_PRIVATE_DIR` or `--private-dir`; the default is `~/.asa/private`, that is `.asa/private` in your home folder on any platform). That folder holds `PURCHASE_MANDATE.md` (section 3), `config.env` (gitignored; template in `examples/config.env.example`) and the raw audit log. `config.env` accepts a **closed list of keys** — `MANDATE_PATH`, `MANDATE_SHA256`, `ALLEGRO_CLIENT_ID`, `ALLEGRO_CLIENT_SECRET`, `ALLEGRO_TOKEN_FILE`, `CDP_URL`, `HUMAN_CONFIRM`, `REF_FULL_NAME`, `REF_DELIVERY_ADDRESS`, `REF_PICKUP_POINT`, the Smart! keys of section 7, the context keys of section 4 (`CONTEXT_STORES`, `CONTEXT_MAX_SNIPPETS`, `CONTEXT_EXCLUDE`, `CONTEXT_BRIEF_MAX_AGE_MIN`) and `ASA_LANG` (`en` by default, `ru` for Russian proposals and mandate lines; Russian input is always understood) — and ignores any other key with a warning, so a key that is not in this list does nothing. The shopping-profile files derived from your knowledge store (section 4) live in `shopping-profile/` in the same folder (not `profile/`, which is the gitignored pattern reserved for browser-profile artifacts with cookies and logins). The Allegro API keys are optional: without them the agent searches through the listing pages of your own logged-in browser session (`asa search --source serp`).

How the agent uses the skill: the runtime does everything that can be checked mechanically (mandate, limits, domains, selectors, audit log, the pay click, the 3DS hand-off) and exits with code `3` and a redacted page snapshot whenever a decision is needed; your Claude Code or Codex session is the "LLM at branch points" that reads the snapshot, decides, fixes a selector if the site changed (`asa selectors:set`), and reruns the step. No separate LLM API key and no third-party browser-agent framework are involved.

If you add permission rules for `asa` commands to your agent harness so that routine steps run without prompts, keep them in a settings file that is not committed to a repository — for Claude Code that is your user-level `~/.claude/settings.json`, or the project-level `.claude/settings.local.json` (local, not committed). Allow only the routine commands (`checkout`, `search`, `select`, `report`; `basket:plan` once it exists), and **never pre-allow `override` or `mandate:*`** — the commands that spend above a limit or change the signed text must always show you a prompt, because the runtime itself has no way to tell your chat confirmation from a claim made by the session (section 3). This is a recommendation for your own configuration; the project ships no permission rules.

## 3. Sign the purchase mandate

The purchase mandate is a short document you write once. It is the reason the model completes the payment instead of stopping at the pay button: it records, in advance and in writing, that you authorize your agent to buy within explicit limits, and the runtime pins its SHA-256 so that the limits cannot be widened silently — any change to the text puts the mandate back to `draft`, and it is valid again only after you confirm the new hash in chat and the agent signs it. **This protection holds only if `mandate:*` and `override` are never pre-allowed in your agent harness:** the session runs those commands itself, and the runtime cannot distinguish your real "ok" in chat from an injected or hallucinated "the user agreed". The prompt your harness shows before such a command is the actual barrier. Why the mandate works with Claude's and Codex's policies is explained in [mandate-spec.md](mandate-spec.md); the template is [`../examples/PURCHASE_MANDATE.template.md`](../examples/PURCHASE_MANDATE.template.md). Write it in your own language if you like — the section numbering 1–7 must stay, and the limit lines in section 2 must follow the parser's format (English or Russian labels are accepted).

**What section 2 says, in plain language** (values are the maintainer's own example; pick yours). These are the lines the parser reads:

| Line in section 2 | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `- Single-item limit: ≤ N PLN` | The most expensive single line in an order. Optional; without it only the order limit applies | ≤ 60 PLN |
| `- Single-purchase limit: ≤ N PLN` (or `Single-order limit`) | The total of one order, items plus delivery | ≤ 100 PLN |
| `- Aggregate mandate limit: ≤ M PLN` | Everything the agent may spend under this mandate, ever; the audit log tracks what is spent | ≤ 600 PLN |
| `- Validity period: from YYYY-MM-DD to YYYY-MM-DD` | Inclusive, Europe/Warsaw; outside it the mandate is void | one month |
| `- Categories: a; b; c` | Exact category strings the agent may buy; anything else is rejected | fasteners; 3D-printing consumables; workshop consumables |
| `- Marketplaces (allowlist): allegro.pl` | Where the agent may act; also feeds the runtime's domain allowlist | allegro.pl |

Two more lines are read by the parser: `- Lines per order: ≤ N` (also `Max items per order`; checked when the runtime knows the number of lines, basket mode is *planned*) and `- One-time approvals up to ≤ N PLN` — the ceiling for a one-time over-limit approval. Without that ceiling line no one-time approval is accepted at all.

Section 3 names the payment instrument by its masked last four digits or "Allegro Pay" — never the full card number. Sections 4–6 are the agent's obligations and its pre-payment checklist; section 7 is the signature.

**Signing.** Signing is a two-message act, deliberately: the agent shows you the hash of the text you are about to sign, you confirm it in chat, and only then the agent writes the signature.

```bash
asa mandate:check --draft          # parses section 2, prints the limits and the SHA-256 of sections 1–6
# you read the limits and answer in chat: "ok <first 8 hex chars of the hash>"
asa mandate:sign --by "Your Name (chat)" --hash <full hash>
asa mandate:check                  # must be green: status signed, hash matches config.env, MANDATE_REVOKED absent
```

`mandate:sign` refuses to sign if the text no longer hashes to the value you confirmed, writes the signer, the timestamp and the hash into section 7, sets `status: signed`, and pins the same hash as `MANDATE_SHA256` in `config.env`. Before every payment the runtime recomputes the hash and compares it with both places. *Current build:* the signature lines and the amended limit lines are written with Russian labels (`Подписано:`, `SHA-256 разделов 1–6:`, `- Лимит одной позиции:` …) even into an English mandate; the checker reads both label languages, so an English mandate stays valid. The runtime's own messages are in English.

**Quick limit changes.** You do not edit the file by hand. Say "item limit 120" or "order limit 150" in chat; the agent runs `asa mandate:amend --per-item 120` (also `--per-order`, `--max-items`, `--total`, `--override-max`, `--from`, `--to`, `--categories "a; b"`), the mandate drops back to `status: draft`, the new hash is printed, you confirm it, the agent signs. Two messages, never one: the confirmation of the hash by you is the only protection against a hallucinated or injected "the user agreed". (`mandate:amend` writes the amended lines with Russian labels; the parser reads both languages.)

**One-time over-limit confirmation.** If the best offer is 72 PLN and your item limit is 60, the agent does not silently drop it and does not silently buy it. It shows the number and says which limit it exceeds. You answer `ok 72` — the amount, not a bare "ok". A bare "ok" when an approval is needed is not understood: the agent repeats the same message and asks nothing new. `ok 72` is recorded by `asa override --amount 72 --by "Your Name (chat)"` for **this run only** (`.state/override.json`, cleared by the next `run:start`, optionally bound to one offer with `--offer-id`, consumed by the mandate gate at checkout steps 8–9). What the runtime enforces: an amount up to the approved value passes the **item and order** checks; the **aggregate limit is never lifted** by an approval — it is the budget's emergency brake, and raising it is a `mandate:amend --total` plus a new signature; and every approval must stay under the **ceiling written into the signed mandate** (`One-time approvals up to ≤ N PLN`) — `asa override` refuses a larger amount, and a mandate without that line accepts no approvals at all. So between an injected "ok 999" and a payment stand the signed ceiling, the aggregate limit, the requirement that the amount comes from you in chat, the rule that `override` is never pre-allowed in your harness, and the hard stops that do not depend on the mandate (a changed recipient, a page total above the selection ceiling, a domain outside the allowlist). Treat every `ok <amount>` as spending real money.

**Revocation.** Create an empty file named `MANDATE_REVOKED` next to `PURCHASE_MANDATE.md`. From that moment `asa mandate:check` is red and **no payment can be made**: the mandate gate at checkout step 8 fails and the pay button (step 9) is never clicked. Note what the current build still does after revocation: `run:start` and `search` only read the mandate id, and checkout steps 2, 4, 6 and 7 still click "Kup teraz", the cart, the delivery option and the payment method before the gate stops the run at step 8 — nothing is paid, but the browser does move. Out of band, freezing the saved card in your banking app stops the money as well. A change of limits is not a revocation — it is the amend flow above.

## 4. Connect your knowledge store

> **Status (2026-09-05): context-first is implemented** and covered by offline tests. The runtime reads your knowledge stores itself through adapters (`CONTEXT_STORES`: an Obsidian vault, the `shopping-profile/` files, any folder of notes), writes a *context brief* for the need at hand (`asa context:brief`) and **refuses to search or plan without one** (`asa search` / `asa basket:plan` exit 2 with the stop reason `context_missing`). The session records what it derived (`asa context:note`), and those facts, assumptions and open questions appear in the proposal. After a confirmed order the runtime appends the purchase to `shopping-profile/purchase-history.jsonl` itself. The profile note and the jsonl files described below stay useful as a curated summary — `asa basket:plan` reads them and `asa profile:check` validates them (it refuses files that carry address-like data) — but they are no longer the only thing the runtime can see. Details: "Context-first" below.

The agent should not ask you what colour, which size, how many, which brand, whether you want an invoice. It should already know. The source of that knowledge is your own Markdown notes: an Obsidian vault, a folder of `.md` files, a wiki export — anything the agent can read as text. The principle in one sentence: **there are no clarifying questions; the only interactions are a one-time over-limit answer and, in basket mode, approving a proposal that adds a complementary item.** A single wishlist line within the limits needs no proposal step. A gap in the data becomes a flagged assumption in the proposal, or the item is left out of the autonomous proposal with one line saying so — never a round of questions.

**What the agent will study.** On first use (and again whenever you ask it to refresh), the agent's session reads your notes and extracts: equipment you own (printer model and nozzle sizes, drone and camera models, tools, vehicles — anything that fixes a specification), consumables and their norms (typical batch, colour, brand, material grade), sellers you have bought from and how it went, past purchases with dates and quantities, and open needs mentioned in project notes. From that it derives, for example, that "M5 nuts" for you means stainless A2 in a pack of 20 because your printer notes say PETG parts with threaded inserts and your last three fastener orders were A2.

**The shopping profile note.** The result is written into one maintained note that you can read and correct, kept under 150 lines, with sections in a fixed order:

0. *How to read* — the order of sources the agent follows when deriving an attribute (your explicit request → the wishlist line → the consumable norm → purchase history → the equipment-derived specification → a flagged assumption) and the zero-question rule.
1. *Delivery and payment, without details* — that you have Smart! (and since when, so the threshold rule is right), your default delivery type (for example an InPost parcel locker; the locker code itself lives only in `REF_PICKUP_POINT` in `config.env`, never in a note), the default payment rail, and whether project purchases need an invoice with a tax number.
2. *Equipment* — one line per device, so specifications can be derived.
3. *Consumables and norms* — a table: `consumable | specification | typical batch | colour | brand | last seller | last purchase | source note`.
4. *Sellers* — trusted (with why) and to avoid (with why: a dispute, an absurd delivery fee).
5. *Needed now (wishlist)* — a table: `item | search query in Polish | category (the exact string from mandate section 2) | quantity | specification | priority | project purchase? | source [[note]]`.

A second, append-only note is the **purchase registry**: one line per order with date, seller, items, amount, and the assumptions that were made. Together the two notes are the whole contract between you and the agent, in a format you own.

**Context-first: what the runtime reads (implemented 2026-09-05).** The runtime opens your knowledge stores itself, through adapters, and the operator session cannot skip that step: it is a gate built into `asa search` and `asa basket:plan`, not a convention in the skill text.

*Setting it up.* In `config.env`: `CONTEXT_STORES=obsidian:C:\path\to\Vault;jsonl:C:\path\to\private\shopping-profile` — semicolon-separated `<kind>:<path>` specs; the kind is everything before the first colon, so Windows drive letters are fine. Kinds: `obsidian` (a vault of Markdown notes with frontmatter), `jsonl` (the four shopping-profile files), `folder` (any folder of `.md`, `.txt`, `.json`). Optional keys: `CONTEXT_MAX_SNIPPETS` (40), `CONTEXT_EXCLUDE` (extra globs such as `Daily/**;Private/**`), `CONTEXT_BRIEF_MAX_AGE_MIN` (240). `ASA_CONTEXT_STORES` in the environment overrides the file for one run. The same config works for a second machine where the vault lives at another path, and a Notion or Logseq export is just another `folder:`.

*What is never read, whatever you configure:* `04 - Archive/`, any note or folder whose name starts with the lock glyph 🔒, dot-folders (`.obsidian`, `.git`, `.understand-anything`, …), `Templates/`, `.jsonl` files inside a vault, files over 512 KB, and notes whose frontmatter says `status: archived` or `done`. Every snippet passes the `REF_*` redaction and is **dropped** if it looks like a postal code, phone number, parcel-locker code, NIP or IBAN. The brief lives in `runtime/.state/` (gitignored, never uploaded, never sent to the marketplace); audit events carry counts and hashes only; the vault path appears nowhere in stdout or the audit log — only `obsidian:<folder name>`.

*How retrieval works.* The session names the need and the terms in the languages of your notes (Russian, Polish, English, sizes, models). Matching is word-boundary and case-folded with cheap folding — `×` ↔ `x`, `180 × 200` ↔ `180x200`, diacritics (`prześcieradło` ↔ `przescieradlo`), inflections (`наволочка` finds `наволочки`, `sheet` finds `sheets`). Each line scores by the terms it contains with a bonus for two or more; a table row is one snippet; the note's frontmatter weights the score (`type: area` / `project` × 1.2, tags with *shopping* × 1.3, `Daily/` × 0.8, `type: map` × 0.7, index and changelog notes such as `AGENT_INDEX.md` × 0.6) and so does recency (1.0 under 30 days, 0.5 after a year). The top snippets (file, line, heading, text, score) form the brief, printed as a digest and saved as `.state/context-brief.json` together with `run.json.context = {brief_hash, need, ts}`.

*The gate.* `asa search` and `asa basket:plan` run only when a brief exists for **this run** (`run:start` deletes the previous one), its hash matches `run.json`, it is younger than `CONTEXT_BRIEF_MAX_AGE_MIN`, and its need covers the need at hand (the same text, or every `--need` label of the basket shares a token with the brief's need or terms). Otherwise: exit 2, stop reason `context_missing`, an audit event `context_gate_stop` with the reason (`no_brief`, `need_mismatch`, `stale`, `run_mismatch`, `no_stores`) and a hint line telling the session to run `asa context:brief`. The only way past the gate is `--no-context "<reason>"`: it needs a written reason, is audited as `context_skipped`, and the proposal header then says "⚠ context not consulted: <reason>". No stores configured at all is the intended failure: `asa context:brief` stops with `no_stores` until you set `CONTEXT_STORES`.

*Walkthrough: a fitted sheet and a pillowcase.* You say "buy a fitted sheet and a pillowcase". The session runs `asa run:start --command "prześcieradło i poszewka"` and then, before any search, `asa context:brief --need "prześcieradło i poszewka" --terms "простыня,наволочка,матрас,кровать,180x200,pościel,prześcieradło,poszewka,bed sheet"`. The runtime scans the stores and prints a digest — for example the row `Кровать · матрасы 90×200 и 180×200` from your home note, `купил наволочки 50×70` from a daily note, `colour: charcoal` from the bedroom project note, and `wishlist:` / `bought …` lines from the profile files. The session reasons and records: `asa context:note --fact "mattress 180×200 → fitted sheet 180×200" --source "[[Дом]]"`, `asa context:note --assumption "colour: charcoal" --reason "the bedroom note names it; no sheet-colour fact"`, and, if the notes are silent, `asa context:note --question "pillowcase size: 50×70 or 70×80"`. Only now `asa search --query "prześcieradło z gumką 180x200 bawełna" --need prześcieradło --append` and `asa basket:plan` run, and the proposal carries "Facts from your notes: …", "Assumptions from your notes: colour: charcoal." and one trailing "not derived: pillowcase size …" line — never a question to you. If the session had skipped the brief, `asa search` would have stopped with `context_missing` and the hint. Exit code 3 from `context:brief` means the stores returned nothing for those terms: the session adds synonyms or records open questions before it searches.

*After the purchase.* Checkout step 10 appends the confirmed order to `shopping-profile/purchase-history.jsonl` (date, seller, title, price, category, offer and order ids), so the next brief already knows about it; the session writes the confirmed facts and assumptions of the brief back into your notes (the profile note and the purchase registry), which stays the human-readable contract.

**Assumptions, shown and written back.** Every proposal carries an "Assumptions from your notes" line — for example "stainless A2/A4, packs of 20/100, invoice: no". When you approve, the session writes those assumptions back into the profile note as facts and moves the wishlist line to "bought", so the next proposal needs fewer assumptions and the profile keeps improving without you maintaining it.

**When data is missing.** Attributes (colour, pack size, grade) may be assumed and are always flagged. Money is never assumed: an amount above a limit is shown as a number and waits for `ok <amount>`; a category that is not in the mandate is not proposed at all; a need with no usable specification gets one trailing line ("could not derive a specification for X, add it to the profile") instead of a question. If you prefer to be asked, that is a change to section 0 of the profile note, not a runtime flag.

## 5. Your browser and payment method

**Your own Chrome, logged in.** The agent works in a real, persistent Chrome profile on your machine and your home network connection, in which you have logged in to Allegro yourself. Field observation (2026-09-03): a fresh browser context with no profile received the DataDome block page on its very first request to allegro.pl; the project does not try to get around that, by design (see [anti-bot-policy.md](anti-bot-policy.md)). Never run the agent headless against a marketplace, never through a proxy, never with Chrome flags that disable the profile's cookie encryption (`--password-store=basic` is a forbidden pattern, see [security.md](security.md)).

**Two browser channels, one at a time:**

| Channel | What it is | When it is used |
|---|---|---|
| **B — dedicated profile over CDP** (default, `--mode cdp`) | A separate Chrome profile started with `--remote-debugging-port=9222 --user-data-dir=<a folder of its own>` (Chrome requires a non-default data dir for remote debugging); the runtime attaches with Playwright `connectOverCDP` (`CDP_URL`, default `http://127.0.0.1:9222`). Log in to Allegro in this profile once, by hand, and leave it open | Every purchase step. Pages are read by the runtime and reach the model only as redacted accessibility snapshots; the delivery screen with your address is checked in-process and never enters the model context |
| **A — your everyday Chrome through the agent's browser extension** (`--mode mcp`) | The session drives the browser itself (Claude in Chrome, or an equivalent) and still uses the runtime for the mandate check, ranking (`asa search --source state`), audit log and report | Recording a flow for a new site, and reading an attribute that is not visible in the runtime's snapshot. Never the address or payment screens: address, payment-method and pay steps run over channel B or are handed to you ([mandate-spec.md](mandate-spec.md) 1.1, item 4) |

`asa browser:check` verifies channel B: connectivity, no block page, logged-in state. `asa ref:capture` reads your name, delivery address and pickup point once **from the checkout delivery page that is currently open in channel B** (it does not launch Chrome and exits with code `3` if that page is not open) and writes them as `REF_*` keys into `config.env` without printing them; during checkout the recipient block on the page is compared with those values in-process (a changed or empty address is a stop — a possible injection or scam).

**Payment: what you already have, nothing new.** The agent pays only with a method already saved in your Allegro account: a one-click saved card (via PayU) or Allegro Pay. It never types card details, never enters a one-time BLIK code, never follows an external payment link, never opens installments ("Raty"). The default rail is the one-click card; Allegro Pay is chosen only when you pass `asa checkout --rail allegro_pay` explicitly (there is no config key for the rail), and the maintainer's own rule is not to do that before five clean card purchases, because Allegro Pay is the most risk-sensitive node of the account ([marketplaces.md](marketplaces.md)). The agent never picks a rail per transaction and never retries a challenged or declined payment on the other rail. The consequence, stated honestly: there is no per-card hard cap; the mandate limits are enforced by the runtime and the append-only audit log.

**One account per user.** Each person runs the agent in their own Chrome profile, with their own Allegro account, their own mandate, their own private folder. Do not share a profile or an account between users or machines, and do not create a second Allegro account for the agent.

## 6. A first purchase, step by step

The first two purchases are done with you sitting next to the browser (`HUMAN_CONFIRM=1` in `config.env`): Allegro may bind the payment method to the device and ask about a "new device" the first time, and that should not happen during an unattended run. From the third purchase on, set `HUMAN_CONFIRM=0` and the agent presses the pay button itself. Because the current build does not yet verify the cart line by line (step 5 below), it is also worth looking at the cart yourself during those attended runs.

1. **You:** "Buy the M5 low nuts from the wishlist." **Agent:** `asa mandate:check` (must be green: signed, in period, `MANDATE_REVOKED` absent, hash matches) and then `asa run:start --command "…"`, which creates a run id and clears the previous run's state. Then, before any search, `asa context:brief --need "…" --terms "…"` and the `asa context:note` lines of section 4 — without a fresh brief the next step stops with `context_missing`. `run:start` itself does not verify the mandate; the verification is `mandate:check` before the run and the gate at checkout step 8.
2. **Search.** `asa search --query "nakrętka niska M5 DIN 439 A2"` (`--source api|serp|state`, `--limit N` to override the order limit) — one page of results. The runtime filters mechanically: Buy-Now only, new items, host allegro.pl, price + cheapest delivery ≤ the order limit and ≤ the remaining aggregate, seller rating ≥ 98 % or Smart! / Super Seller; it ranks by total cost ascending, Smart! and parcel-locker offers preferred on ties. The session checks each title against your specification and records why the chosen offer matches (`asa select --id … --category … --rationale "…"`). Product cards on Allegro group several sellers, so the runtime opens the offer page to read the actual offer id. *(Planned: a `--need` link to the wishtlist line, exclusion of avoided sellers, and a stop when the seller on the offer page differs from the plan — none of these are implemented; today the session compares the seller itself.)*
3. **Proposal.** Today the proposal is the session's own chat message: the selected offer, its total with delivery, the limit check and the assumptions it made; no runtime command produces it. *In basket mode (planned)* `asa basket:plan` will print one message and write `.state/basket-plan.json`, like this:

   ```
   Proposal #a1b2c3 · SELLER_A (Smart!, bought here twice before) · InPost locker · one-click card
   1. Nakrętka niska M5 DIN 439 A2, 20 pcs — 12.50   [need: M5 low nuts]
   2. Nakrętka wbijana M5 DIN 1624, 100 pcs — 17.00  [need: MultiBoard]
   Items 29.50 → 20.40 short of the 49.90 Smart! threshold.
   3. (+) Rym-bolt DIN 580 M5 A4 ×4 — 23.70  [bought from this seller before; tier 1]
   A: with 3 = 53.20 + delivery 0 = 53.20 zł  ← default     B: without 3 = 29.50 + 11.99 = 41.49 zł
   Alternative to 3: 4) Nakrętka DIN 985 M5 A2 ×50 — 21.90 [tier 2]
   Assumptions from your notes: stainless A2/A4, packs of 20/100, invoice: no.
   Limits: item ≤60 ✔ · order ≤100 ✔ · lines ≤4 ✔ · mandate remaining 600.00.
   Reply: "ok" (=A) · "ok B" · "ok A/B" (A, fall back to B if Smart! does not apply) · "ok without 3" · "ok + 4" · "no" · "item limit 120"
   ```

   (The seller name and the 11.99 zł delivery are placeholders, not a real seller or a Smart! rate.)
4. **You answer.** Today the only answer the runtime records is an over-limit approval: `ok <amount>` → `asa override --amount <amount> --by "Your Name (chat)"`; "item limit N" / "order limit N" is the amend flow of section 3; "no" ends the run. *In basket mode (planned)* `asa basket:approve --reply "…"` will parse `ok`/`A`, `ok B`, `ok A/B`, `ok without N`, `ok + N`, `ok <amount>`, `no` and the limit phrases, and a bare `ok` when an over-limit amount is required will get the same message again with no new questions. Approving a proposal is a cart-mandate step before checkout (AP2 Intent → Cart), not a payment confirmation, and it will be needed only when a complement is added or an amount exceeds a limit; a plain wishlist purchase within the limits runs without it. `asa checkout` starts from the offer recorded by `asa select` (`.state/selected.json`); one offer per run today, the multi-line cart path is planned.
5. **Checkout, one step per call** (`asa checkout --step 1..10`, details in [`../skills/allegro.pl/flows/checkout.md`](../skills/allegro.pl/flows/checkout.md)). Every step first checks the domain allowlist and the block-page markers. Step 1 opens the offer page and checks the logged-in state; step 2 clicks "Kup teraz"; **step 3 reads the total shown on the page and stops with `mandate_deviation` if it exceeds the ceiling recorded at selection** (the offer price plus its cheapest delivery) — that is the whole cart check today: it does not compare offer ids, quantities or seller groups and removes nothing, so an auto-added paid service that keeps the total under the ceiling would be paid; step 4 moves to the delivery screen; step 5 compares the recipient block with your `REF_*` values in-process; step 6 clicks the delivery-option selector (its CSS is still marked "verify" in `selectors.yaml`) and reads no delivery cost; step 7 selects the payment method for the rail; step 8 is the full mandate gate on the total shown by the shop (and the `HUMAN_CONFIRM=1` stop); step 9 runs the gate again, clicks "Kupuję i płacę" and waits for the payment outcome; step 10 reads the order number. *(Planned: line-by-line cart verification — same offer ids, quantities, exactly one seller group, subtotal and delivery line; detection and removal of auto-added services such as purchase protection or extra warranty; reading the delivery cost at step 6.)*
6. **What the agent reports.** `asa report`: item, seller, amount, why this offer, the rail used, the remaining aggregate limit, the order link (delivery cost and whether Smart! applied: planned). The audit log gets `order_confirmed` and the runtime appends the order to `shopping-profile/purchase-history.jsonl`; the session appends the purchase registry note, moves the wishlist line to "bought" and writes the confirmed facts and assumptions of the context brief back into your notes.

**When it stops, and what you see.**

| Situation | What happens | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Bank 3DS / SMS challenge after the pay click | The browser leaves allegro.pl for the bank's page. The runtime reads and clicks nothing there, records `challenge_3ds{start}`, prints "3DS: confirm the payment in your banking app (waiting up to 5 minutes)" and waits | Approve in the bank app. The runtime resumes on return; a timeout is a stop (`3ds_timeout`, order awaiting payment), never a retry on any rail |
| CAPTCHA, DataDome block page, logged-out session, re-authentication prompt | Exit code `2`: the run stops and the reason is logged; nothing is retried or worked around | Solve it yourself in the browser if you wish, then start a new run |
| Deviation from the mandate or the selection (page total above the selection ceiling, recipient or address differs from `REF_*`, mandate gate red) | Stop with `mandate_deviation` / `mandate_red` (exit `2`); the agent makes a **new proposal**, it never substitutes an item on its own | Answer the new proposal |
| A page the skill does not recognise (a changed checkout, an unresolved selector, a total or order id not found) | Exit `3` with a redacted snapshot in `.state/`; the session fixes `selectors.yaml` or asks you | Usually nothing; sometimes confirm the fix |
| `HUMAN_CONFIRM=1` | The run stops at step 8 before the pay button with status `human-confirm` | Press "Kupuję i płacę" yourself in the browser window |

The complete list of stop triggers (off-platform payment requests, requests for secrets, injection-like text, scam score, irreversible account actions) is in [security.md](security.md) §5.

## 7. Allegro Smart!: how the agent will get free delivery

> **Status (2026-09-04, evening).** The planner exists: `asa basket:plan` groups candidate offers by seller, applies the threshold, proposes complements and prints the one-message proposal; `asa basket:approve` parses your reply; the settings at the end of this section are read from `config.env`. **Still planned:** buying more than one basket line per run, reading the actual delivery cost at checkout (`smart_threshold_observed`) and searching a seller's own shop for complements — today complements come from the other needs you searched with `--need`. The facts paragraph below is current.

**Facts** (sources: Allegro's own announcement in Allegro Pomoc, <https://allegro.pl/pomoc/aktualnosci/zmiany-w-allegro-smart-od-2-marca-darmowa-dostawa-przy-zakupach-od-49-90-zl-u-jednego-sprzedajacego-jn6Ww9nd1i7>, and the press release <https://media.allegro.pl/446561-smart-jeszcze-prostszy-allegro-wprowadzi-jeden-prog-darmowej-dostawy-dla-wszystkich-przesylek-allegro-smart>, both read 2026-09-04): since 2026-03-02 the free-delivery threshold is **49.90 zł of purchases from one seller**, the same for parcel lockers, pickup points and courier; before that it was 45 zł for lockers and pickup points and 65 zł for courier; subscriptions activated or renewed before 2026-03-02 keep the old thresholds until the end of their current subscription period, then move to the new one (the planned profile note records your `smart_since` date so the right threshold is used). Consequences: two 30 zł items at two different sellers pay delivery twice; two 25 zł items at one seller ship free. Field observation (2026-09-03): the "X zł z dostawą" price on a product card is the item plus the seller's cheapest delivery **without** Smart!, so the runtime treats it as a ceiling, not as the expected total. **Hypotheses, not verified against the Smart! terms:** that both the offer and the chosen delivery method must be Smart!-marked, and that the order must be paid through Allegro (a one-click card and Allegro Pay both qualify) — [marketplaces.md](marketplaces.md) only records that Smart! is applied automatically at checkout. Conditions you may have read elsewhere (a cap on the seller's total delivery cost, per-method caps, no cash on delivery) were not found in the sources above and are not relied on. Coupons: the agent never applies them; whether they reduce the amount counted towards the threshold is unverified and does not matter to the planner. Between an item limit of 60 and an order limit of 100 the practical Smart! window is 49.90 ≤ items ≤ 100, that is one to three items at one seller.

**How the planner will use that (`asa basket:plan`, pure functions in `basket.ts`, state in `.state/` — planned):**

1. **Prefer Smart! offers.** Candidates that are not Smart!-marked lose the ranking tie-break; sellers you bought from before get a bonus (`bought_before`); avoided sellers and over-limit offers are rejected outright.
2. **Consolidate at one seller.** Offers are grouped by seller login (read from the offer page, not the product card). For each seller the planner computes the subtotal, whether every line is Smart!, the expected total (subtotal + 0 if the threshold is reached, otherwise + the cheapest delivery), the ceiling (subtotal + every line's cheapest delivery, used as the hard limit at checkout), and the gap to the threshold. The plan with the lowest expected total that covers all needs wins; on a tie one seller beats split orders, then `bought_before`, then Smart!, then company seller, then rating. A plan that would break your item, order or line limit is not chosen automatically — it is shown with its numbers and marked `needs_override`, never trimmed silently.
3. **Fill the gap with something you buy anyway — with your approval.** Only when the gap is small (`0 < gap ≤ SMART_SLACK_PLN`, default 25 zł) and no other wishlist line fits at that seller, the planner looks inside that seller's own shop for a complementary item, in three tiers: (1) items you bought from this seller before (same offer id, or same seller and a near-identical title), (2) lower-priority wishlist lines, (3) one generic query in the primary item's category, accepted only with ≥ 50 ratings and only if it costs less than the delivery it saves. Every candidate must be Smart!, cost between the gap and the gap plus slack, stay under the item limit, keep the order under the order limit, be in a mandate category (out-of-mandate candidates are not shown at all), not be on your do-not-buy or "have enough" list, and not have been bought in the last `REORDER_COOLDOWN_DAYS` (30) unless it is a consumable. At most three candidates are shown; at most `MAX_COMPLEMENTS` (default 1) is added, and the mandate itself caps complements at two. The proposal always shows both variants — A with the complement and free delivery, B without it and with paid delivery — and you choose with `ok`, `ok B`, `ok without 3` or `ok + 4`. If the gap is larger than the slack or nothing qualifies, only variant B is offered, or a low-urgency need is held for a later order.
4. **Verify at checkout, do not assume.** At delivery selection the runtime will read the actual delivery cost and log `smart_threshold_observed{subtotal, delivery, threshold}`. If free delivery was expected and the page shows a cost, the run continues only if you approved `ok A/B` and the total is still under the ceiling; otherwise it exits with code `3` and asks. It never pays a different total silently. If delivery turns out free between 45 and 49.90 zł, the runtime records that your subscription still has the old threshold (`SMART_THRESHOLD_PLN=45`). (Today step 6 reads no cost; the ceiling check at steps 3, 8 and 9 is the only protection.)

Planned settings for `config.env` (not accepted by the current build — setting them now does nothing): `SMART_THRESHOLD_PLN` (default 49.90), `SMART_SLACK_PLN` (25), `MAX_COMPLEMENTS` (1), `REORDER_COOLDOWN_DAYS` (30).

## 8. OLX later

OLX.pl has no buyer API and no saved payment method, so the OLX skill (next after Allegro; not in the repository yet) will be browser-only and deliberately narrow: **only listings with the native "Kup z Przesyłką OLX" button** (PayU escrow, 72-hour seller confirmation, buyer protection package). The agent will search listing pages in your own logged-in session, rank inside the mandate, run the escrow form up to the payment step — and there **you confirm the payment yourself** (a BLIK code, a bank transfer authorisation or card entry with a possible 3DS challenge, all on your side, never from the agent's context). Listings without delivery will be skipped and reported; chat and negotiation are a v2 feature with human escalation; paying outside the escrow, following an external link or moving to WhatsApp are prohibitions, not risks to weigh. A scam detector (fresh account, anomalous price, off-platform contact, external domain, urgency) will stop the run when its score reaches the threshold. After delivery, the escrow release ("Przedmiot jest OK" / "Mam problem") needs you to inspect the parcel; the agent will remind you and record the outcome. Details: [marketplaces.md](marketplaces.md) §2 and [security.md](security.md) §2.

## 9. Honest limits and risks

- **Marketplace terms.** Allegro's Terms (Regulamin, edition effective 2026-09-01, read 2026-09-03) prohibit the use of bots and other software tools while using Allegro (art. 10.11) and scraping of its data for reuse (art. 10.10), state that automated solutions are used at the user's own risk (art. 2.8), and allow Allegro to block such tools (art. 8). An AI agent driving your own browser may fall under art. 10.11. Allegro offers no buyer API. OLX's terms likewise prohibit automated data extraction and OLX blocks accounts for atypical activity. The project does not disguise the agent and does not defeat any platform control; **you decide and accept this risk for your own account**, and the human-confirm flag exists if you want a human step before every payment. This is not legal advice; a Polish legal opinion is an open item ([architecture.md](architecture.md), open question 10).
- **Anti-bot stops, no bypass — ever.** A DataDome block page, a CAPTCHA, a logged-out session or a re-authentication prompt ends the run. The agent does not solve challenges, does not rotate anything, does not change its pace to avoid detection, and does not retry. Hygiene is not evasion: one account, your own profile and connection, one action at a time, a few purchases a day ([anti-bot-policy.md](anti-bot-policy.md)).
- **The bank's 3DS challenge cannot be removed** (PSD2 strong customer authentication). How often it appears on small saved-card payments is the project's first metric and is still being measured; expect to be asked sometimes, and expect Allegro Pay to be the more sensitive rail ([payments.md](payments.md)).
- **No hard cap on the card.** The limits live in the mandate, the runtime and the audit log, not in the bank. A one-time `ok <amount>` lifts the item and order limits up to the ceiling you signed, never the aggregate limit (section 3). An erroneous purchase inside the limits is yours (mandate section 5). Keep the aggregate limit at what you can afford to lose to a bug, never pre-allow `override`, and keep `MANDATE_REVOKED` one keystroke away.
- **The cart is checked by its total, not line by line** (section 6, step 5). Until line-by-line verification ships, an auto-added service that stays under the selection ceiling would be paid; attended first runs and a quick look at the cart are the mitigation.
- **The agent reads untrusted pages.** Product descriptions, seller names and messages are treated as data, never as instructions, secrets never enter the model context, and actions are confined to allowlisted domains — but no injection detector is complete. Defence in depth (mandate, allowlist, secrets outside the model) bounds the damage; it does not make it zero ([security.md](security.md)).
- **Your notes will be the specification.** Once the profile reader exists, a wrong norm in the profile note buys the wrong pack size, within the limits, without a question — that is the price of zero clarifying questions. Read the "Assumptions" line of every proposal; correcting the profile note is a one-line edit.
- **Early software, one marketplace.** Allegro selectors are verified by the maintainer from a personal profile, not by CI (Allegro's terms permit no automated smoke runs); a site redesign can break a step until `selectors.yaml` is patched. OLX is next, not now. Purchases above roughly 130 zł are outside the MVP's expectations for challenge-free payment.
